# Key-People Share Plan - Strategy v1

**B-Catcher Group** | 04 - Organisation & Headcount | Version 1 | 23 November 2026
How B-Catcher Group uses equity to attract senior people from established finance firms (C-suite, senior brokers, platform leaders) **while the founders always keep at least 51% of the parent company**. The figures come from *BCatcher_Group_Integrated_Financial_Model_v10.xlsx* (Funding & Returns sheet). This is a planning document. It is not legal or tax advice, and the structure needs sign-off from a lawyer and a tax adviser.

---

## 1. The rule: founders never go below 51% of B-Catcher Group

| Founders' ownership of the parent | With Seed + Series A + Series B | With Seed + Series A only |
|---|---|---|
| Before any share plan | 52.2% | 57.0% |
| **After the 1.5% group executive pool (modelled)** | **51.1%** ✔ | 55.8% ✔ |

- Every 1% of parent shares set aside for staff before Seed costs the founders about 0.75% by the end of Series B. With all three rounds, the founders have only about 1.6% of headroom.
- **So most of the equity for recruits is given in the subsidiaries, not the parent.** Honey Pot Pty Ltd and BHive Pty Ltd each set aside 15% for key people. B-Catcher Group keeps 85% of each and **100% of the votes**.
- The model has a **founder control check**, which now reads "OK - founders 51.1%". If any future round or grant would take the founders below 51%, it shows "BREACH".
- **Shareholders' agreement protections:**
  - Any share issue that would take the founders below 51% needs founder consent.
  - Founders have pre-emptive rights on new issues.
  - The parent share pool is capped.
  - All subsidiary key-people shares are non-voting.

The subsidiary stakes are deducted from the exit value: about $5.5m, or 3% of the $177m equity value. After both, the Seed return is **6.4x (~45% IRR)**, Series A **3.7x** and Series B **2.9x**.

## 4. Terms (to attract people and protect the company)

- **Vesting:** 4 years with a 1-year cliff, then monthly. Sign-on grants for people leaving a big firm can vest faster in year 1, to replace bonuses or shares they give up ("buy-out awards"). Buy-outs can be part cash, paid over 2-3 years.
- **Broker vesting hurdles:** Honey Pot partner equity vests on **tenure plus quality**, not volume alone:
  - placement volume, measured over 3 years
  - clean file reviews
  - no compliance breaches
  - investor retention

  This keeps the scheme clear of conflicted-remuneration risk. The ban applies to advice to retail clients, and the group serves only wholesale clients at launch, but rewards should still not look like sales commissions.
- **Leavers:** a good leaver keeps vested equity, and the company can buy it back at fair value. A bad leaver forfeits vested equity, or it is bought back at the lower of cost and fair value. The company can claw back equity for misconduct.
- **Change of control:** unvested equity accelerates only if the holder is also let go after a sale (double trigger).
- **Getting value out of subsidiary shares:**
  - **Sale or IPO of the group:** the shares are bought for cash at an independent valuation, or swapped into parent shares, but only if the founders stay at or above 51%.
  - **From year 5:** holders and the company have put and call options at fair value.
  - **Drag-along and tag-along rights** apply, so a buyer can take 100%.
- **Fair dealing between companies:** the agreements between the three companies (B-Catcher's shared-services fee to the subsidiaries and BHive's platform licence fees) must stay at arm's length. Otherwise the minority holders of Honey Pot and BHive could argue they are being treated unfairly. Document these agreements and have the board review them every year.

## 5. Tax and legal points to confirm (Australia)

- **Startup tax concession for employees (Division 83A, ITAA 1997):** for unlisted companies under 10 years old with turnover under $50m.
  - It covers options with an exercise price at or above market value, or shares issued at a discount of 15% or less, held for 3 years.
  - Tax is usually deferred until sale and charged as capital gains, often with the 50% discount.
  - This needs an independent market valuation at each grant round. That is now budgeted at $1,500 a month in the model.
- **Employee share scheme rules (Corporations Act Part 7.12, Division 1A):** these give disclosure and licensing relief for offers to employees and eligible contractors of unlisted companies. Offers where staff pay money are subject to value caps.
- **Brokers who are not employees** may not qualify for the startup tax concession. Get specialist advice before making Queen-tier offers. Options include a simple loan-funded share plan, or performance rights taxed on vesting.
- **Subsidiary shares** need their own share plans, constitutions with a non-voting B class, and valuations. The set-up budget already includes $40k for the shareholders' agreement and share plan. Add about $25k for the two subsidiary plans when they are adopted.

## 6. Options if more equity is needed at the parent

1. **Skip or replace Series B.** In the Base case, Seed and Series A ($14m) cover the $11.7m peak funding need. Cash then falls only about $0.3m below the required safety buffer plus buy-back reserve, so a slightly larger Series A (~$8.5m) or a small bridge loan would replace Series B. Without Series B, the founders hold about 55.8% (with the 1.5% pool). That leaves room for up to **~8% more** in the parent pool while staying at or above 51%. The Downside case would still need more money.
2. **Raise Series B at a higher valuation**, or make it a convertible note, so the founders give up less.
3. **Non-voting parent shares (dual class).** Do this only if the 51% rule is about **control** (votes) rather than economic ownership. Staff could then hold non-voting parent shares without touching the founders' 51% of the votes. Please confirm which one you mean.
4. **Phantom equity** for anyone else: a cash bonus linked to the group's value at exit, with no shares and no votes.

## 7. Decisions for the founders

1. Confirm the 51% rule means **51% of the economic ownership** (modelled) or **51% of the votes**. Votes only would allow a larger staff pool through non-voting shares.
2. Approve the 1.5% parent pool and the 15% pools in Honey Pot and BHive.
3. Approve the Queen-tier broker equity programme, subject to the conflicted-remuneration and tax opinions.
4. Decide whether Series B is needed. Skipping it frees room for parent equity.
